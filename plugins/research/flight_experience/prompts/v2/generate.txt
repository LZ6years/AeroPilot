Extract useful flight guidance from original observations, requested actions,
actual execution feedback and subsequent views. Each experience addresses ONE
decision question. Identify what was directly observed, what was requested versus
executed, and which interpretation remains uncertain. Do not retell the flight.
Keep the smallest supported advice that could change a future observation choice,
motion magnitude or stopping decision. State applicable conditions and situations
where it cannot transfer. Failure can expose a useful limitation; success is not
required. Numbers and action sequences usually belong in evidence, not a universal
recipe. Do not assert correct identity from a detector label or self-confirmation.
Reject generic advice with no concrete decision value. Return up to the supplied
candidate limit, including zero. References must include a supplied original RGB
and an actual timeline event supporting the action/result relationship. Cite no
image you have not received. Evidence IDs are local to their source. Use temporary
candidate IDs c1, c2, ...; the application owns durable identity and publication.
Every ID in observation_ids MUST also appear verbatim in evidence_refs, alongside
at least one supplied timeline event ID. Listing a photo only in observation_ids
does not satisfy the citation contract. Never invent an ID to complete these fields.
Name an observable trigger for changing the next decision. A sequence of shorter
motions does not prove it maintained clearance; stable altitude does not prove
that altitude was safe. Do not supply a purpose or benefit the evidence does not
establish. If only generic advice or an unsupported success story remains, return
an empty candidates array. A limited observation-checking rule is preferable to
an untested action prescription. State missing outcome evidence explicitly.
Synthetic example: a requested displacement was clipped; advice may concern using
measured feedback to estimate the next window, not always repeating that movement.
