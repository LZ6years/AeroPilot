# Approach And Completion Checks

## Qualitative And Numeric Relations

A nearby hold may be supported by target identity, visual relations and measured
approach change. Do not add exact image centering to a qualitative request. A
specified metric separation instead needs valid evidence for the relevant target
geometry; surface depth is not automatically horizontal or center-to-center range.
Empty or invalid measurements leave distance unknown, not zero.
An earlier measurement remains evidence about its original viewpoint. Account
for subsequent vehicle and target motion, geometry and uncertainty before using
it to estimate current separation. A qualitative request does not introduce an
exact numeric threshold; an approximate distance retains its stated tolerance.

For an approach-and-hold task, a nearby tree and doorway may identify the intended
building before leaving view. They need not both remain visible at the requested
hold if target and scene continuity remain supported. Extra motion merely to
recover that descriptive background is unnecessary. An inspection photo explicitly
requiring both features still needs simultaneous visibility. A following task needs
updated evidence as the target moves; an earlier satisfied separation does not
establish continued satisfaction.

## Landing And Task Order

Before descent, establish the requested region and surface with usable clearance.
Shorten near a surface, preserve specified path components while applicable and
stop when the instructed relation is supported or further descent adds unsupported
risk. Descent alone does not prove touchdown. Follow the stated completion contract.

A final target may be visible while an earlier passage or turn condition remains
unresolved. Check the full instruction and retained route history before ending.
Confirmed passage can survive a landmark leaving view; do not require returning to
reacquire every cue. Correct contradicted interpretations rather than treating
successful action execution as proof of navigation success.

## Above A Roof

A roof visible ahead identifies a possible destination but leaves the vehicle's
horizontal relation unresolved. Check the building identity, retained route and
roof edges before choosing a short approach. Compare actual translation with the
roof footprint's changing relation to the vehicle. An enlarged roof or an ascent
alone does not place the vehicle over its footprint. If the available camera view
cannot establish that relation, keep it uncertain and choose a supported observation
position; do not invent a downward view or unseen clearance.

## In Front Of A Building

Yaw puts a facade at image centre without changing the vehicle's physical side.
The requested front may be the entrance side, while the vehicle faces a side wall.
Check the entrance, facade corners, access path and adjacent landmarks. A short
lateral move may reveal the entrance and separate overlapping walls. Its resulting
view and measured translation can test the front-side relation. Centring the building
alone cannot establish it; a surface measurement needs compatible geometry to
answer any requested separation.

## At The Route Endpoint

The final courtyard is visible after passing a gate, but the entrance remains ahead.
Keep the supported gate passage and verify the courtyard's identity. A short move
can test whether the entrance recedes and the required courtyard position becomes
supported. Visibility is not entry. If an earlier turn is still uncertain, inspect
the retained route evidence before ending; proximity to a candidate final landmark
does not substitute for the instructed route conditions.
