# Route Progress Examples

Use these examples only when the core route guidance is insufficient to
interpret a side passage, gap, or disappearing route cue.

## Passing A Landmark On One Side

Treat the requested side as a spatial route relation. First establish the
landmark and plausible free space, then use lateral or forward translation that
preserves the requested side. A changed camera bearing caused only by yaw does
not establish the side relation.

## Passing Beyond A Route Cue

After directionally consistent translation, a cue may move from ahead to the
edge, behind, or outside the current view. When the path ahead remains usable
and movement agrees with this change, there are two valid evidence patterns:
the cue may visibly move to the side or rear, or it may leave the forward view
after the route relation was established. The second pattern does not require
reacquisition unless motion reversed or another observation contradicts it.

## Crossing A Gap Or Opening

Use the boundaries of the opening to establish a plausible path, but do not
wait for perfect symmetry or centering. Completion needs meaningful motion
through the opening plus a later view that supports being beyond the constricted
region.

## Ambiguous Progress

At a suspected road end, a facade ahead may be distant or may hide a bend. Use
successive road-edge views and any lateral continuation to distinguish a true
terminal node from occlusion or an ordinary intersection. Seeing the node is not
arrival. After entering the instructed continuation, conflicting route evidence
calls for reassessment rather than treating an executed turn as proof of success.
A visible surface's depth can guide a bounded approach to that surface; it is not
automatically the distance to the turn location. Check the lateral continuation
and the UAV's relation to it. A blocked view after turning can have several causes;
choose an observation that distinguishes them before changing the route hypothesis.

Along a confirmed straight segment, compare actual prior translation and visual
change to choose meaningful progress before the next observation. Approaching a
junction or an uncertain continuation shortens that horizon.
Execute one physical action, inspect its resulting observation, and then select the next action.

If a cue changes image side without meaningful translation, treat the change as
camera reorientation rather than traversal. If translation occurred but the
latest view and motion disagree, stabilize and obtain a new observation before
choosing another substantial movement. Absence by itself is inconclusive; use
the previously established route relation and accumulated translation together.

## Seeing, Reaching And Entering A Junction

An exit is visible beyond a stretch of road; this establishes a candidate junction,
not arrival. A short move should bring the opening toward the vehicle's lateral
position and reveal connected road edges. Compare that action's measured motion
and images before treating the turn condition as reached.

After arrival, turn toward the connected road and inspect the resulting view.
If further alignment is needed, adjust the heading and inspect after each turn.
Facing the connected road
still does not prove entry. Translate into it, then check whether road boundaries
and the former junction's receding position support the new route segment. If the
opening stays ahead or a foreground wall fills the view, revisit the arrival judgment.

## Passing A Cue Before The Endpoint

The instruction says to pass a shelter and stop at a courtyard beyond it. Coherent
translation moves the shelter alongside and out of view. Keep that supported
passage, but check the courtyard's identity and required stopping relation before
ending. A later identity correction need not erase independently confirmed passage;
retain the reliable relation and explicitly revise the uncertain one.
