# Choosing An Observation Position

## Occlusion Or Bearing

A foreground canopy hides a continuation. Turning without translation may reveal
another bearing but leave the same obstruction in front. A rise, retreat or side
shift can change the geometry; choose one supported by clearance and constraints.
Predict the feature to reveal and inspect the resulting image. If it stays hidden,
revise the explanation instead of repeating the same move.

## Low Detail And Competing Candidates

When low detail leaves the image, descent or backing away may expose it. When two
similar objects overlap, lateral parallax can separate foreground and background.
Investigate the relevant attributes before committing to an identity. A fixed
camera is not tilted by moving the vehicle; changing position changes its view.

## Useful Enough

Facing an identifiable target with a usable forward bearing need not mean exact
centering. Once the requested detail or route relation is visible, resume its
judgment. If an acquired view becomes dark or otherwise unusable, reconstruct the
last clear view and actual actions with abnormal recovery instead of continuing
route movement through missing evidence.

## Similar Appearance Is A Candidate

Two red-roofed buildings fit a colour cue, but the instruction names one beside a
road bend and across from a small shelter. Compare these surrounding relations
with retained route history before choosing. A side shift may separate the buildings
and expose the bend; predict which relation should appear, then check the new view.
Colour, detector confidence or a valid surface distance alone cannot settle identity.

## A Viewpoint Test Does Not Reveal The Expected Feature

A rise intended to reveal a road behind a canopy leaves the same canopy dominant.
Compare the actual height change with the view: little actual movement means the
test was not performed as expected; substantial rise with unchanged obstruction
weakens the proposed geometry. The road may lie elsewhere, or the obstacle may
extend farther than assumed. A short retreat or lateral shift can test parallax
where clearance supports it. Reinterpret the obstruction before repeating a climb;
one unsuccessful test does not prove the road absent.
